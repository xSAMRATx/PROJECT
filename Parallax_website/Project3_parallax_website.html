Parallax Website

Section One

Section one starts the journey. Scroll slowly and watch the background stay still while the content moves over it. This effect is called parallax scrolling, and it is made here with only HTML and CSS: each section has a full-screen background image with background-attachment set to fixed, so the image stays in place while the page scrolls. It is a simple trick, but it gives a page a feeling of depth and makes long pages more fun to explore. Nature photos work especially well because wide landscapes look great at full screen. Try resizing your browser to see how the layout keeps the image covering the whole section on any screen size.

Section Two

Section two is about exploring nature. Forests, rivers and mountains remind us to slow down and look around. When you build a page like this, choose large, high-quality images and compress them so the page still loads quickly. Keep the text short and readable on top of the image: a semi-transparent background behind the text helps it stand out from busy photos. Good contrast and enough spacing make the difference between a pretty page and a page people actually enjoy reading. Small details like smooth scrolling between sections also make the experience feel polished without any JavaScript.

Section Three

Section three looks deep into nature. The quieter the scene, the more details you notice: light through the leaves, mist over the water, the shape of a mountain at sunset. Web design works the same way. A clean layout with fewer elements lets the important parts shine. This project was a practice in CSS backgrounds, positioning and full-height sections, and it taught me how much a simple effect can add to a page. If you want to build your own, start with three sections, three images and a few lines of CSS, then experiment until it feels right.
